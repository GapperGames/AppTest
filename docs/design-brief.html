How it works

Four stages, camera to cloth

The trick: all the aiming maths happens in a clean, flat top-down view of the table — never in the distorted camera image.

A

Find & lock the table

Detect the felt rectangle, then let you drag the four corners to sit exactly on the cushions. This one human touch removes the biggest source of aiming error and builds the top-down coordinate map.

B

Find the balls

Inside the felt, non-cloth blobs are balls. Because we know how big a ball should look, false positives mostly vanish. Each ball gets a precise position and a type — cue, solid, stripe, or a snooker colour.

C

Solve the shot

Pure geometry: the ghost-ball method for direct pots, the mirror trick for banks, plus checks for cut angle, blocked paths, and whether the pocket is even makeable.

D

Draw it on the table

ARCore keeps the overlay glued to the real cloth as you move: aim line, ghost ball, contact spot, the ball's path, and a difficulty read-out.

The core idea

The ghost ball

To sink a ball, it must set off straight toward the pocket. So the cue ball has to strike it on the exact opposite side. The ghost ball is where the cue ball's centre needs to end up at the moment of contact — one full ball-width behind the object ball, away from the pocket.

G = O + 2r · unit(O − P)

Aim the cue ball straight at G, and the object ball rolls toward the pocket P. Everything else — the cut angle, bank shots, difficulty — builds on this one line.

The build plan

Eight phases, each one runnable

No big-bang reveal. Every phase produces something you can hold and judge. The aiming maths gets proven on a laptop before the camera is ever involved.

P0

Skeleton

App runs; ARCore locks onto a surface.

Done when: a marker stays put as you move.
P1

Calibrate

Corner-drag calibration builds the top-down map.

Done when: a test grid lies flat on the cloth.
P2

See the balls

Detect and label pool balls, live.

Done when: balls get the right highlight, steadily.
P3

The maths

Ghost-ball engine in pure Kotlin, unit-tested.

Done when: tests are green and the aim line agrees with your eye.
P4

Make it look right

Full overlay: ghost ball, contact spot, path, difficulty.

Done when: a stranger reads the shot at a glance.
P5

Bank shots

Mirror-trick banks + obstruction checks.

Done when: a one-cushion path draws and verifies.
P6

Snooker mode

Six colours + the tricky pack of reds.

Done when: colours ID and reds give usable lines.
P7

Polish

Freeze mode, settings, robustness in real lighting.

Done when: you reach for it on your own table.

Straight talk

Where it's honest about its limits

  • —It's an aid, not a guarantee. It gives the perfect line; you still have to deliver a straight stroke.
  • —Bank shots are estimates. Real cushions bend the angle with speed and spin. Banks carry a clear "estimate" label.
  • —You can't cue while filming. So there's a freeze step — line it up, freeze, then set the phone down and play.
  • —Fairness. Aiming aids are banned in most competitive play. This is for practice and casual games.
